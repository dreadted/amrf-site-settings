Below is a high‑level outline of how you can break your single “Admin Panel Settings” page (which currently shows both your General Settings section and your User‑Role
Settings section in one form) into two completely separate forms (and even two separate sub‑pages under “Settings” if you like).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1) What you have today

Currently you register one options‑page (amrf‑admin‑settings) and in page_init() you add two settings sections (“General Settings” and “User Role Settings”) to that
same page. Then, in create_admin_page(), you output one big form that renders all of your fields:

    public function create_admin_page()
    {
        $this->options = get_option('amrf_admin_settings', $this->default_settings);
    ?>
        <div class="wrap">
            <h1><?php _e('Admin Panel Settings', 'amrf-admin-settings'); ?></h1>
            <form method="post" action="options.php">
                <?php
                settings_fields('amrf_admin_settings_group');
                do_settings_sections('amrf-admin-settings');
                submit_button();
                ?>
            </form>
        </div>
    <?php
    }

## 2) The goal: two separate forms (and even two pages)

You essentially want to split that big form into:

    1. **General Settings** form
    2. **User Role Settings** form

and optionally you may want each one on its own Settings sub‑page rather than both on one page.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3) Two common approaches

### A) Two separate Settings pages

    1. **Register two options pages** instead of one.
    2. **Register each section** on its own page‑slug.
    3. **Output two distinct forms**, each targeting its own page‑slug (and its own submit button).

Rough outline:

    public function add_admin_menu() {
        // Page #1: General Settings
        add_options_page(
            __('Admin General Settings','amrf-admin-settings'),
            __('Admin General','amrf-admin-settings'),
            'manage_options',
            'amrf-admin-settings-general',
            [$this, 'create_general_admin_page']
        );

        // Page #2: User‑Role Settings
        add_options_page(
            __('Admin Role Settings','amrf-admin-settings'),
            __('Admin Roles','amrf-admin-settings'),
            'manage_options',
            'amrf-admin-settings-roles',
            [$this, 'create_role_admin_page']
        );
    }

【✏】you’d replace your single add_options_page('amrf-admin-settings', …, 'create_admin_page') call with two of the above.

Then in your page_init() you would register settings sections/fields like this:

    // General Settings hook into slug 'amrf-admin-settings-general'
    add_settings_section(
        'general_settings_section',
        __('General Settings','amrf-admin-settings'),
        [$this, 'print_general_section_info'],
        'amrf-admin-settings-general'
    );
    // … all your general add_settings_field() calls also use page 'amrf-admin-settings-general'

    // User Role Settings hook into slug 'amrf-admin-settings-roles'
    add_settings_section(
        'user_role_settings_section',
        __('User Role Settings','amrf-admin-settings'),
        [$this, 'print_user_role_section_info'],
        'amrf-admin-settings-roles'
    );
    // … your per-role add_settings_field() calls use page 'amrf-admin-settings-roles'

【✏】i.e. change the fourth argument of each add_settings_section / add_settings_field to the matching page slug.

Finally, create two page callbacks:

    public function create_general_admin_page() {
        $this->options = get_option('amrf_admin_settings', $this->default_settings);
        ?>
        <div class="wrap">
          <h1><?php _e('General Settings','amrf-admin-settings');?></h1>
          <form method="post" action="options.php">
            <?php
            settings_fields('amrf_admin_settings_group');
            do_settings_sections('amrf-admin-settings-general');
            submit_button(__('Save General Settings','amrf-admin-settings'));
            ?>
          </form>
        </div>
        <?php
    }

    public function create_role_admin_page() {
        $this->options = get_option('amrf_admin_settings', $this->default_settings);
        ?>
        <div class="wrap">
          <h1><?php _e('User Role Settings','amrf-admin-settings');?></h1>
          <form method="post" action="options.php">
            <?php
            settings_fields('amrf_admin_settings_group');
            do_settings_sections('amrf-admin-settings-roles');
            submit_button(__('Save Role Settings','amrf-admin-settings'));
            ?>
          </form>
        </div>
        <?php
    }

【✏】These replace your single create_admin_page().

### B) One page, two tabs (two forms on one screen)

If you’d rather keep one menu entry but have two tabs (General vs Roles), you can:

    1. Read a `tab` query‑arg (`$_GET['tab']`) to figure out which tab is active.
    2. In your single callback (`create_admin_page()`), output a tab‑nav, then conditionally show one of two forms:

    $current_tab = isset($_GET['tab']) && $_GET['tab'] === 'roles'
                 ? 'roles'
                 : 'general';
    ?>
    <h2 class="nav-tab-wrapper">
      <a href="?page=amrf-admin-settings&tab=general" class="nav-tab <?php echo $current_tab==='general'?'nav-tab-active':'';?>">
        <?php _e('General','amrf-admin-settings');?>
      </a>
      <a href="?page=amrf-admin-settings&tab=roles" class="nav-tab <?php echo $current_tab==='roles'?'nav-tab-active':'';?>">
        <?php _e('User Roles','amrf-admin-settings');?>
      </a>
    </h2>

    <?php if ($current_tab === 'general'): ?>
      <form method="post" action="options.php">
        <?php
        settings_fields('amrf_admin_settings_group');
        do_settings_sections('amrf-admin-settings-general');
        submit_button();
        ?>
      </form>
    <?php else: ?>
      <form method="post" action="options.php">
        <?php
        settings_fields('amrf_admin_settings_group');
        do_settings_sections('amrf-admin-settings-roles');
        submit_button();
        ?>
      </form>
    <?php endif; ?>

【✏】you’d still split your add_settings_section() calls to register into two distinct page slugs (amrf-admin-settings-general vs amrf-admin-settings-roles) exactly as
 shown in approach A, but you render them conditionally on one screen.

### Summary

    1. **Decide** whether you want two **separate menu entries** under Settings, or one entry with two **tabs**.
    2. **Split** your calls to `add_settings_section()` and `add_settings_field()` so that General fields go under page‐slug `amrf-admin-settings-general` and Role
fields under `amrf-admin-settings-roles`.
    3. **Output** two different `<form>…</form>` blocks (one per slug) in your admin‐page callback(s).
    4. **Ensure** your sanitize/merge logic doesn’t clobber the section you’re *not* saving at that moment.

That will give you two completely independent forms (and even two navigation entries, if you like). Let me know if you need any more detail or example snippets!
